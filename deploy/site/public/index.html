RUST, ALL THE WAY DOWN

Serious engineering.
Uncomplicated feeling.

The complicated bit is our job. Rust for speed and efficiency, from native binaries on your machines to WebAssembly in your browser. One shared protocol keeping them in step.

01 / THE SHORT WAY HOME

Close when it can be.
Connected when it can’t.

On the same machine, Roost tries loopback. Further away, it tries an authenticated WebRTC connection. If a direct route isn’t available, the coordinator carries your terminal over WebSocket.

Direct connections depend on your network. Roost doesn’t require one to work.

02 / ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH

Your terminal stays
on the same page.

The terminal engine runs on the worker. Your browser paints its cells instead of interpreting a second stream of terminal commands. Ordered frames and fresh snapshots keep reconnects in step.

Retained scrollback loads when you ask for it, not every time you open a tab.

03 / A SAFE PLACE TO PERCH

A keeper for
the things still running.

A separate keeper process owns your shell sessions, so restarting a worker or updating the coordinator doesn’t have to end your work. Your programs stay on the machine where you started them.

The host still needs to stay awake and running. Roost isn’t a backup for your files.

A few things you might be wondering.

Before you settle in.

Is this a terminal emulator or a multiplexer?

Both, across a browser and your machines. A Rust terminal engine interprets your programs’ output on each worker; the web app renders the screen, sends input, and lets you arrange multiple sessions in tabs and split panes. You can still run tmux inside it.

Does it work with coding agents?

Yes. Install and run your preferred coding CLI just as you would in any terminal. Roost doesn’t install agents, manage their accounts, or turn their interface into a chat page. Your tools stay your tools.

Do I need Tailscale?

No. Local use needs no VPN. To reach Roost from another device, use private Tailscale access or your own HTTPS front door. Tailscale isn’t enabled merely by installing Roost: roost access --mode tailscale previews the setup and asks you to approve it. It requires an installed, authenticated Tailscale client, Caddy, and the documented host prerequisites. Your phone must join the same tailnet and be paired with Roost.

Read the access requirements ↗
What happens when I close the browser?

Your terminal process keeps running on its host. Reopen Roost to reconnect. A dropped connection isn’t an instruction to end the shell. The host must stay awake; a host shutdown, process exit, or unrecoverable failure can still end a session.

Where does my work live?

On the machines you connect. You host the coordinator and workers; Roost doesn’t move your programs into a vendor’s cloud sandbox. Traffic can pass through your coordinator when a direct route isn’t available. Third-party programs and optional services you configure have their own data policies.

Can I really use it on my phone?

Yes. iPhone and Android use the same web app, with touch selection, a command composer, terminal keys, and swipe navigation. Pair the browser over a reachable HTTPS address. Add Roost to your home screen for an app-like window; on iOS, that’s also required for push notifications.
